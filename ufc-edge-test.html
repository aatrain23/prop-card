Age is the strongest fighter-level signal. In near pick'em fights the younger fighter returned +7.8% (2010–21) and +10.7% (2022–26); the older fighter lost 16% and 19%. Fighters 35+ facing someone under 32 lost about 21% since 2022. Long layoffs and two-fight skids also hurt underdogs. Reach and stance did not hold up.

Method props are a tax. Every method market lost money; favorite by decision was the least bad at -2.8%. Stick to moneylines unless you have a specific read.

The model

A logistic model starts from the market's no-vig probability and adjusts for age gap, reach gap, takedown gap, year-plus layoffs (both sides), UFC debuts and two-fight losing streaks. Trained on 2010–2021, tested on 2022–2026 only:

How this report works

Picks are scored against a model trained on 6,916 UFC fights with closing odds (2010–2021) and tested on 1,899 fights it never saw (2022–Mar 2026). A pick needs at least one SportsLine or Action Network analyst on it and none on the other side; the model filters and sizes those picks, and can add a half-size play on a fight no analyst picked. This is an early run: lines were pulled Friday Nov 20 around 10:30 PM ET, about 12 hours before the first fight, as the median of seven sportsbooks on BestFightOdds (Action Network's odds screen couldn't be reached, so there are no opening lines this week). Props are BestFightOdds medians across the sportsbooks listing each prop (two to four books), read Saturday about 12:15 AM ET. Analysts this week: SportsLine's Daniel Vithlani, Kyle Marley and Josh Nagel (read signed in, Saturday about 12:25 AM ET), plus Action Network's Sean Zerillo (articles, logged picks and moneyline projections), Dan Tom, Billy Ward (article and logged picks) and Ian Undery (read Saturday about 12:40 AM ET).
